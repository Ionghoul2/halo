# Selling this

## The pitch

Thirty seconds, in their language:

> You're selling to pharmacies and labs. Right now they order from you over
> WhatsApp and pay you through an app. Their compliance officer can't sign off
> on that, so you're losing the accounts that buy the most. I'll put you on a
> portal where a buyer applies with their licence number, you approve them, and
> they order against a quote with a real invoice. Two weeks. Here's what it
> looks like.

Then send the demo link. Don't describe it — let them click.

than the whole build.

## Client intake checklist

Before you take money from a supplier, get all four. A legitimate supplier
produces these in ten minutes and is glad to be asked.

- [ ] **Business entity name and EIN** — you invoice a company, not a person
- [ ] **FDA establishment registration number (FEI)** — any facility supplying
      API into the US supply chain has one
- [ ] **A certificate of analysis for any two products** — proves the lab work
      behind the catalogue exists
- [ ] **Two or three named customer facilities** — "three compounding
      pharmacies in Texas" is enough

If a prospect stalls on these or says they don't do that, walk. Keep the demo,
find the next one. There are more suppliers than you have hours.

## Terms that protect you

Put these in the agreement. They're normal and nobody will blink.

1. **They own the domain and the hosting account.** You get collaborator
   access. Your name is not the registrant.
2. **They supply all product content** — descriptions, pricing, COAs,
   compliance copy — and warrant they have the right to sell it.
3. **You build and maintain software.** You don't sell, store, handle, ship or
   take payment for product. Say it in one sentence in the contract.
4. **Payments go to their processor, their bank.** Never route a client's
   customer payments through your account. Not once, not as a favour.
5. **Either side can terminate on 30 days.** You keep the code; they keep
   their data.

## Where to find clients

In rough order of how fast they say yes:

1. **Suppliers already emailing PDF price lists.** They've shown they want to
   sell online and have no way to. Ask for the price list, then send back the
   demo with their products already in it. That close rate is high enough to
   justify the hour it takes.
2. **Lab and ingredient distributors at trade shows.** Their websites are
   usually a decade old. Badge list is public.
3. **Contract manufacturers.** They sell to brands, same gated-pricing problem.
4. **Anyone whose site says "contact us for pricing" with a mailto link.** That
   link is the entire sales pipeline of a real business. You can replace it.

## First week

- **Day 1** — Share the demo link. Send to five prospects.
- **Day 2** — Build a second demo with a real prospect's catalogue in it. Send
  it to them personally. This is the move that closes.
- **Day 3-5** — Follow up by phone. Email alone gets ignored by this industry.
- **Day 5-7** — First setup fee collected.

Three Standard clients is $10,500 up front and $1,350/month recurring.
